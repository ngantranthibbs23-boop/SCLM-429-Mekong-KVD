# 03. D2C Strategy – Multichannel vs. Omnichannel

## 1. Project Background

Mekong KVD planned to expand from its traditional B2B wholesale business into a Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) online sales channel.

However, its products, including fish sauce, soy sauce, and cooking oil, are packaged in heavy and fragile glass bottles. This creates challenges related to delivery costs, packaging, and customer experience.

The project evaluated two D2C fulfillment strategies: Multichannel and Omnichannel.

## 2. Multichannel Strategy

Under the Multichannel model:

- Online orders are processed independently from existing retail partners.
- All online orders are fulfilled directly from the Mega Hub.
- Inventory is centrally managed.
- The model provides better centralized control but may result in longer delivery distances and higher shipping costs.

## 3. Omnichannel Strategy

Under the Omnichannel model:

- Online sales are integrated with existing retail partners.
- Selected partner locations operate as Micro-Fulfillment Centers (MFCs).
- Customers can choose home delivery or Buy Online, Pick Up In-Store (BOPIS).
- Inventory information is synchronized to support order routing.

This model uses the existing retail network to bring products closer to customers.

## 4. Strategy Comparison

| Evaluation Criteria | Multichannel | Omnichannel |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery speed | Slower | Faster local fulfillment |
| Inventory management | Centralized | Multiple locations |
| Customer options | Home delivery | Home delivery and BOPIS |
| Operational complexity | Lower | Higher |
| Partner commission | No partner commission | 25% commission |

## 5. Financial Analysis

The project compared the contribution margin of both fulfillment models.

| Model | Contribution Margin | Margin % |
|---|---:|---:|
| Multichannel | 75,000 VND/order | 25% |
| Omnichannel | 45,000 VND/order | 15% |

Although Multichannel provides a higher contribution margin per order, Omnichannel offers faster local fulfillment and more convenient pickup options.

## 6. Phase 1 Omnichannel Network

The proposed Phase 1 network includes:

- 2 Micro-Fulfillment Centers (MFCs).
- 3 BOPIS pickup points.
- Integration with the existing retail partner network.
- Inventory synchronization through API integration.

The order fulfillment process includes three paths:

1. Standard D2C: The nearest MFC fulfills the order and arranges home delivery.
2. BOPIS: Customers collect their confirmed orders at a selected pickup point.
3. Fallback: If nearby MFCs cannot fulfill the order, the system checks other locations or routes the order to the Mega Hub.

## 7. Minimum Order Value (MOV)

The project recommended a Minimum Order Value of 2 bundles (600,000 VND).

The financial analysis showed:

- 1 bundle: 45,000 VND contribution margin (15%).
- 2 bundles: 150,000 VND contribution margin (25%).

This recommendation helps improve per-order profitability by spreading fixed delivery and packaging costs across a larger order.

## 8. Final Recommendation

The project recommended the Omnichannel model for Phase 1.

Although it has a lower contribution margin per order than Multichannel, it provides faster fulfillment, BOPIS convenience, and better use of the existing retail network.
